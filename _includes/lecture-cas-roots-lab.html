Determinants, spin-adapted configurations and electronic roots are not the same thing

Two electrons distributed among two spatial orbitals give four spin orbitals and six Slater determinants. For the singlet sector there are three spin-adapted configuration-state functions (CSFs). Each singlet root is a different eigenvector—a linear combination of those CSFs—of the same Hamiltonian within this symmetry sector.

The three singlet CSFs are |g²⟩, |gu; S=0⟩ and |u²⟩. The middle CSF is itself a spin-adapted combination of two open-shell determinants.

\[ \lvert \Psi_k^{S=0} \rangle = \sum_{I=1}^{3} c_{Ik}\lvert \Phi_I^{S=0}\rangle, \qquad \mathbf H_\mathrm{CAS}\mathbf c_k=E_k\mathbf c_k. \]

What changes between roots? The coefficient vector \(\mathbf c_k\), energy \(E_k\) and electronic state. The determinants/CSFs form a basis for representing each root; they are not three different excited-state roots. Under state-averaged CASSCF, selected roots share optimized orbitals but have different CI eigenvectors.

Model boundary: the 3×3 Hamiltonian and slider are an invented dimensionless coupling model designed to illustrate state mixing, not CASSCF energies for an actual molecule. Singlet and triplet roots belong to different spin-symmetry sectors in the spin-free treatment; this plot shows singlets only.
