# Luca Gerhards
### *Theoretical chemistry · spin dynamics · quantum biology · multiscale modelling*

I am a theoretical chemist and **Carl von Ossietzky Young Researcher Fellow** at the Institute of Physics, Carl von Ossietzky University Oldenburg. My research connects electronic-structure theory, open quantum dynamics and multiscale molecular modelling to understand **spin-selective chemistry, magnetic-field effects, electron-transfer processes and magnetic-resonance observables in complex molecular and biological systems**.

A central goal of my work is to connect microscopic quantum dynamics with experimentally measurable phenomena in realistic environments. This includes radical-pair reactions, spin relaxation, photo-CIDNP and hyperpolarization, flavin photochemistry, cryptochromes, biomolecular electron transfer, and the influence of molecular motion on spin dynamics.

I am the **lead developer of [MolSpin](https://molspin.eu)**, an open-source molecular spin-dynamics framework designed for general and extensible simulations of spin systems. My methodological work focuses on scalable descriptions of large and open spin systems and on connecting molecular structure and dynamics to quantum-spin observables.

## Current research

- **Spin chemistry and open quantum dynamics** — radical pairs, magnetic-field effects, spin relaxation and stochastic spin dynamics.
- **Multiscale biomolecular physics** — linking molecular dynamics, electronic-structure calculations and spin dynamics.
- **Hyperpolarization and magnetic resonance** — photo-CIDNP, NMR/EPR observables and reaction-yield detected magnetic resonance.
- **Flavin and cryptochrome photochemistry** — electron transfer, radical-pair formation and magnetosensitivity in proteins.
- **Electronic-structure theory** — DFT/TD-DFT and multireference methods for ground, excited and spin states.
- **Scientific software** — algorithms and numerical methods for high-performance spin-dynamics simulations.

## Recent highlights

My recent work includes a 2026 review on **multiscale modelling in biomolecular physics**, reaction-yield detected magnetic-resonance spectroscopy of cryptochrome radical pairs, spin-dynamics studies of flavin–tryptophan systems, photo-CIDNP applications and investigations of magnetic-field effects in chemical and biological environments. In 2025, I led a *Chemical Reviews* article on weak radiofrequency fields and the radical-pair mechanism.

In 2026 I also taught at the international **Spin-Chemistry** summer school and gave an invited lecture at the German Federal Office for Radiation Protection (BfS) on the relevance of the radical-pair mechanism for radiation protection.
